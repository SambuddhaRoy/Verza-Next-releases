verza

A music player for your computer and your phone. It plays YouTube Music without the ads and downloads in lossless. Flick the phone at your screen and the song carries on from the computer.

get it see it first

Free. No ads, no Verza account, nothing tracked. Windows, Linux and Android.

the cover picks the colour

now playing

Verza has one accent colour, and the record chooses it. When a song starts, the app takes the strongest hue on its cover and adjusts it until text in that colour reads at 4.5:1 against the page.

Behind the cover is the cover again, warped and blurred, and it moves with the bass. The bars around it are the spectrum of what you're hearing.

This page does the same. Each section has a colour, and the bar at the bottom changes with it.

phone and computer

flick it over

or drag across the drawing

You're listening on the phone and you sit down at your desk. Flick the phone toward the screen. The computer takes the song at the second it had reached, and the phone goes quiet.

Flick it back toward yourself and the song returns to your pocket.

the queue comes too

Up to 500 songs, counted from the one playing. If something was already playing where the song lands, the arrival wins. A setting makes it ask first.

about 150 ms

A song nobody expected takes around ten seconds to start. So whatever your phone starts, the computer loads too, and a handover plays almost at once.

verza connect

Pair once with a six-digit code. After that the phone shows what the computer is playing and can pause it, skip it, or hand it a queue.

no server

The two talk to each other over your Wi-Fi and to nobody else. Windows asks about its firewall the first time you turn Connect on.

downloads

the best copy there is

Music / Coldplay / Parachutes / Coldplay - Yellow.flac

YouTube has no lossless audio, so Verza looks elsewhere. Turn on Download in lossless and a download becomes a FLAC from Soulseek, where people share their own rips. Verza makes the account for you.

On the computer the highest resolution wins, 24-bit ahead of CD. The phone prefers CD quality to save space. A file much smaller than the other copies of the same song is an MP3 dressed up as a FLAC, and Verza skips it.

Each file gets its tags and cover from MusicBrainz and lands in your Music folder by artist and album, where any other player can open it. A song nobody is sharing downloads from YouTube instead, about 4 MB.

When a song you play is already on the disk, the file plays instead of the stream.

home and your sound

made from what you play

home

Recently played, on repeat, favourites you'd forgotten, and mixes that start from an artist you've had on lately. Verza builds them on your own machine from your own plays. One mix follows the time of day.

discovery radio

Radio that stays in the genre you started from and plays only songs you haven't heard. It remembers the last 5,000 it offered, so it doesn't loop back to the same forty.

your sound

How much you listen, when, and to whom. It also names your genre, using MusicBrainz's curated genres, and builds a radio from it.

your account, if you want

Sign in to YouTube Music and your likes, playlists and Home come along. A like in Verza is a like on your account. Skip the sign-in and the rest still works.

verza for android

in your pocket

The phone app came first, and the desktop copies its design. It has the same four words across the bottom, the same Now Playing, the same downloads into the same kind of folders.

It plays in Android Auto and from the lock screen. Five home-screen widgets and a quick-settings tile control it. Share a YouTube link to it from any app and it plays the song.

The spectrum around the cover is read from the player's own output, so Verza never asks for the microphone.

windows will warn you

The installer isn't code-signed, because a certificate costs a few hundred dollars a year. SmartScreen says so for any installer it doesn't know. Choose More info, then Run anyway.

so will android

Verza isn't on Google Play, so Play Protect hasn't seen the file before and offers to scan it. Let it, then install. You also allow your browser to install apps, once.
